Print-in-Place, Explained: Why Articulated Toys Need No Assembly

2026-09-045 min read

Print-in-place is the party trick of 3D printing: a jointed dragon comes off the bed already flexing, no glue, no hinges, no assembly. The magic is not magic — it is clearance, carefully modeled.

The mechanism: modeled air gaps

Every articulated link is separated from its neighbor by 0.3–0.5 mm of designed air. The printer lays down link A, bridges the gap with a thin curved roof, then lays down link B. Done well, the parts never touch during printing — and never fuse afterward. Done carelessly, they weld into a souvenir.

Why the first layer decides everything

Squished first layers swell outward in every direction, and 0.1 mm of extra squish is enough to close a 0.3 mm joint. This is why a perfectly calibrated first layer matters more for flexi models than for any brick. If a joint seizes, blame the calibration before the file — see the first-layer guide.

The three commandments

  • Orientation is sacred. The designer placed joints for a reason; rotating the model "to fit the plate better" is how hinges die.
  • No supports near joints. Support material inside a 0.4 mm gap is removal surgery.
  • Slow outer walls help. Joint zones print cleaner at moderate speed with cooling on.
Free movement test: flex every joint right after the bed cools. A stiff link now is a broken link later — reprint while the settings are fresh in mind.

Where to see it in action: the whole Flexi Toys & Learning collection, dragons and skeletons in Dragons & Fantasy, and even spooky season has its own jointed skeletons.
