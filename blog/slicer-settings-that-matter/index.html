The Only Slicer Settings That Really Matter

2026-09-046 min read

Slicers expose two hundred settings so that you can safely ignore one hundred ninety of them. These are the ones that change prints; everything else is decoration until you have a specific reason.

Layer height — the trade everyone understands

0.2 mm is the daily driver: decent speed, decent look. 0.12–0.16 mm earns its long print time on display pieces with fine relief — figurines, layered scenes, anything judged up close. Going below 0.1 mm on a 0.4 mm nozzle mostly buys frustration.

Walls beat infill, always

Strength comes from perimeter loops, not from honeycomb. Three walls and 12% infill handles decor; holders and trays that get gripped daily want four to five walls. If a part feels floppy, add a wall before you add infill — same stiffness, better surface, often less material.

Top and bottom layers

Four to five solid top layers hide infill completely on flat tops. Saggy or pillowed top surfaces mean not enough layers or too sparse — an easy fix that instantly upgrades organizers and coasters.

Cooling and temperature, briefly

Fan at 100% for PLA overhangs; dialed back for tall, thin parts that get brittle. Temperature is per-filament — run one temp tower per brand and stop guessing forever.

The anti-tweaking rule: change one setting per print. If you cannot say what changed, you cannot say what worked.

Where each choice shows up: crisp geometry on desk organizers, smooth relief on figurines and planters, durable hinges on flexi toys.
