How to Prepare a 3D Model for Printing: A Step-by-Step Checklist

2026-09-046 min read

Most failed prints are not slicer problems — they are model problems that only show up at the nozzle. Run any file through this checklist, in this order, and the success rate jumps immediately.

1. Start from a sound mesh

A printable model has to be watertight: every edge shared by exactly two faces, no holes, no flipped normals. Marketplaces usually curate this, but free downloads and self-made scans often are not. Quick checks: run the file through the analysis pass in your slicer, or open it in Meshmixer and hit repair. If the slicer reports "non-manifold" or "holes detected", fix before slicing — never hope.

Rule of thumb: if the preview looks wrong — missing walls, infill floating inside solid areas — trust the preview, not your optimism.

2. Confirm scale and units

STL files carry no units, and some models arrive in inches that look like millimeters. Drop a 20 mm calibration cube next to the import, or sanity-check a known detail — a phone stand should be roughly phone-sized. For anything with moving or mating parts, add 0.2–0.4 mm of clearance per side.

3. Respect wall thickness

With a standard 0.4 mm nozzle, keep walls at least 1.6 mm — ideally a multiple of your line width. Thin sculptural fins, like feathers on angels or petals on vases, are fine as long as they carry no load.

4. Choose orientation deliberately

  • Strength: layers are the weak plane. Load-bearing parts want forces running parallel to layers, not across them.
  • Supports: orient overhangs so supports touch faces you will hide or sand later.
  • Print-in-place: articulated models have baked-in clearances. Print them in the documented orientation — rotating them "for a better fit" usually welds the joints solid.

5. Slice with intent, not defaults

0.2 mm layers are the honest default. Drop to 0.12 mm for display pieces with fine relief; raise infill from decorative 10–15% to 30%+ for anything that holds weight. Tall, thin parts want a brim. Prints that must survive handling deserve 3–4 wall loops.

6. Supervise the first layer, then walk away

Ninety percent of prints die in the first ten minutes. Level the bed, set the Z-offset, watch two layers go down, only then leave. Ready to test the checklist? Pick something small from Keychains & Craft Tools, or go straight for the crowd-pleasers in Flexi Toys & Learning.
